About

What I work on, and how I work.

Who I am

South Korean Computer Science graduate from UCL, now pursuing an MSc in Artificial Intelligence and Medical Imaging in UCL's Department of Medical Physics & Biomedical Engineering. My work sits where 3D computer vision meets clinical imaging: I have co-authored multiple paper submissions to both top-tier computer vision venues and clinical, domain-specific venues, and my MSc thesis focuses on 3D reconstruction of deformable anatomy from minimally invasive surgical video. I am building a career in machine learning for medical imaging, where models have to hold up on real patient data.

How I work

Labeled medical data is scarce and real clinical images are noisy, so I lean on self-supervised and physically grounded methods, and I test on realistic clinical data, not only clean benchmarks. I work closely with supervisors and domain experts so each project stays tied to a real clinical need. Client projects and military service taught me structured planning and clear communication. Outcomes matter, but sound process matters more.

Selected Works

IPCAI submission · first author

SEAMLESS: Specularity-Aware End-to-End Adaptive Masking and Luminance Estimation via Self-Supervision

Self-supervised monocular depth and pose estimation for GI endoscopy that learns to separate luminance, specular reflections, and tissue albedo end to end, removing the handcrafted specularity detector used by SHADeS. Visibly cleaner depth on real EndoMapper colonoscopies.

−16%AbsRel vs SHADeS on C3VD (0.1312 → 0.1104)
−19%AbsRel vs SHADeS on the more realistic C3VDv2 (0.1423 → 0.1156)
